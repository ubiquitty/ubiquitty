## Allowed without permission

- **Factual reference.** You may use the name to refer to the project
  truthfully: "works with Ubiquitty", "a Ubiquitty theme", "compatible with the
  Ubiquitty Sync protocol", "embeds the Ubiquitty terminal engine", or in
  articles, reviews, tutorials and package listings.
- **Unmodified redistribution.** You may redistribute official Ubiquitty
  releases, or builds of an unmodified tagged source release, under the name
  and icon, provided you follow the licenses and do not suggest that you are
  the project or are endorsed by it.
- **Packaging changes.** Distribution packagers may make the changes needed to
  build, install and integrate an unmodified release with their system (paths,
  dependency versions, build flags, security patches backported from upstream)
  and keep the name, as long as the package description says it is
  repackaged.
- **Libraries by description.** A product that embeds the Apache-2.0 terminal
  components may say so ("terminal powered by Ubiquitty's engine") but must not
  use Ubiquitty, or a confusingly similar name, as its own product name, and
  must not use the logo or icon.

## Requires a different name and icon

A fork, a modified build, or any product built from changed source must use a
name and icon that are not confusingly similar to Ubiquitty's. This includes:

- builds with functional changes beyond the packaging changes above;
- builds distributed under a different bundle identifier, update channel or
  signing identity than the official release while still presenting the name;
- names that combine or vary the mark, such as "Ubiquitty Pro", "Ubiquity
  Terminal", or "UbiquittyX".

Keep the copyright and license notices; it is only the branding that changes.
You may say truthfully that your fork is "based on Ubiquitty".

## Never allowed

- Suggesting that the project or its author sponsors, endorses or supports your
  product or service when it does not.
- Using the marks in a domain name, account name or app-store listing in a way
  that could be mistaken for the official project.
- Using the logo or icon in a modified form, or as part of your own logo.

be trademarks of their respective owners. They are used only to identify those

Their use implies no affiliation with, sponsorship by, or endorsement by their
